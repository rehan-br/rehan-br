## A bit about me

By day, I ship the kind of software that isn't allowed to break — multi-tenant platforms handling real patient data, real money, and real people who never see the codebase and shouldn't have to. That means a lot of time thinking about who's allowed to see what, and making sure the *database* enforces that answer, not just the UI pretending to.

By night, I build stranger things. A race engineer that talks to me over radio while I drive a video game and only speaks when something actually changed. A recommender that learned my taste from raw audio instead of trusting a genre tag someone else assigned. A word game that tells the truth almost all the time — which is exactly what makes it dangerous.

I like systems with a heartbeat: things that have to make a call in real time, under real constraints, without a human standing by to double-check.

Currently: founder & lead engineer at **The Developer Guys** — and still the person who reads the whole stack trace before asking for help.

<br>

## What I've been building

**🏎️ A voice race engineer for F1 25**
Listens to the game's live telemetry and calls strategy the way an actual pit wall does — tyres, fuel, pit windows, incoming threats. The hard part was never the voice. It was teaching the thing *when to shut up*: a bank of stateful situations that speak on onset and on real change, and stay quiet the rest of the time.

**🏥 ClinicOS**
Day-to-day software for small multi-specialty clinics, built around one rule: authorization lives in the database, not in a component that forgot to check a role. Front desk can't read medical notes. Doctors can't see the money. Neither has to trust the other's honesty — the data layer just won't hand it over. Bilingual dictation turns spoken Urdu/English clinical notes into an editable draft, never an auto-saved fact.

**🎧 A music taste model that skips the genre tag entirely**
Embeds a personal library with audio foundation models, crawls outward for things I've never heard, and ranks discoveries against a taste model trained on what I actually *finish* listening to — not just what I clicked. Built three competing models and a hold-out harness honest enough to let the fancier one lose.

**💊 VitaCommerce**
A supplements storefront where checkout is the easy part. The real problem lived in the warehouse: stock tracked lot by lot with real expiry dates, allocated oldest-first automatically, every unit traceable back to the batch and supplier it came from.

**🧪 Smaller, finished things**
A word game where the tile colors lie to you on a hidden schedule. A constellation tracer that hands your shape to an LLM and gets a myth back. A sketchpad you draw in with your hands, no mouse involved. Small, live, done.
